## Engineering Bulletin: Closed-Loop Thermal Architecture

### The WayMaker Project vs. Hyperscale “Water-Cooled” and “District Heating” Models

#### 1. Working Fluid and Hydronic Dependency vs. a Completely Waterless Footprint

**Monolithic “closed-loop” water centers:** Although marketed as “closed,” these facilities circulate large volumes of treated stormwater, municipal greywater, or chemical coolant through extensive pipe networks. They require ongoing chemical treatment (including biocides, anti-corrosion additives, and scaling inhibitors), system flushing, and makeup water to compensate for drift or leakage.

**The WayMaker architecture:** Uses waterless internal convective airflow and a sealed phase-change heatsink. It draws no water from municipal systems, requires no retention basins or chemical biocides, and produces no wastewater or chemical blowdown effluent.

#### 2. Concentrated 300 MW Thermal Plumes vs. Distributed Corridor Micro-Dissipation

**Monolithic “closed-loop” water centers:** Concentrate 50–300+ MW of continuous thermal energy on a single parcel. Dry-chiller installations can require large roof radiator arrays and fan banks that exhaust air at 110–135°F or higher, creating localized heat and adding parasitic fan load to the grid.

**The WayMaker architecture:** Distributes computing into independent modular nodes rated at approximately 2.0 kW each (about 6,824 BTU/hr) along rail rights-of-way. Dispersing heat generation across miles of track, rather than concentrating it on one site, is intended to allow dissipation into ambient corridor airflow and avoid localized hotspots.

#### 3. Subsurface Heat Pollution and Microbial Disruption vs. Above-Grade Ballast Protection

**Hyperscale “district heating” thermal routing:** Trenching hot-water conduits through streets can radiate heat into surrounding earth. This may warm shallow aquifers, reduce dissolved groundwater oxygen, dry root zones, and alter soil microbiology.

**The WayMaker architecture:** Maintains an above-grade profile mounted on rolling rail stock and engineered track ballast, with no trenching through native topsoil or intended injection of thermal energy into the earth. The design therefore avoids direct thermal routing into municipal aquifers, water tables, and soil ecosystems.

#### 4. The “Lukewarm Heat Pump” Electric Penalty vs. Passive Convective Efficiency

**Hyperscale “district heating” thermal routing:** Server cold plates may discharge fluid at 95–115°F, while some building-heating and domestic-hot-water applications require higher temperatures, often 140–160°F. Booster heat pumps can be needed to raise the temperature, consuming electricity from the grid.

**The WayMaker architecture:** Rejects heat at the point of generation using stack-effect convection between bottom track-skirt intake louvers and roof exhaust cowls. Low-voltage 48 V DC brushless fans, powered by onboard solar and battery storage, supplement natural draft. The design targets a thermal-management parasitic load below 5% and does not depend on utility power for cooling or community-funded heat pumps.
